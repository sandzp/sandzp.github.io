AI in medicine

Before I moved into building production AI/ML systems full-time, this was where I wrote and published as a clinician-researcher: what artificial intelligence and machine learning could (and couldn't yet) do for surgery, neurosurgery, and clinical decision-making.

  • The Surgical Singularity Is Approaching

    Sandip S Panesar

    Scientific American (Observations)·December 27, 2018

    AI-powered robots may soon be doing some procedures faster, more accurately, and with fewer complications than humans. Drawing a parallel to Moore's Law and the collapsing cost of genome sequencing, the piece argues that continued technological progress could make autonomous surgical systems affordable and widespread — a "surgical singularity" where AI-assisted robots become the standard for surgical intervention.
